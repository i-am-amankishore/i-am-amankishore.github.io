TruKKerAgentic AI

EVA, Fatima and Sara

I designed and implemented an agentic AI framework across the freight lifecycle. Its named agents, EVA, Fatima and Sara, are not pilots. They carry real volume every day.

What changed

  • 85% of customer inquiries automated by EVA
  • 30% improvement in sourcing efficiency, from Fatima
  • 25% improvement in operational efficiency, from Fatima
  • 28% fewer escalation cases, from an AI operations assistant I built
  • 8,600+ AI-based onboardings across customers, suppliers, drivers and assets

The problem. Agent projects rarely fail on the model. They fail on a decision nobody owns: what the agent may do alone and what it must hand to a person.

What I did. The framework covers the lifecycle: demand capture, supply matching, pricing, onboarding and operations. I own the AI transformation roadmap. I also built an AI operations assistant and scaled AI-based onboarding across customers, suppliers, drivers and assets.

My rule: an agent gets an escalation path before it gets a launch date.

The rule is easy to state and easy to break. Try the router: switch off the money guardrail and watch the release gate fail.

Try the router

Classification is a commodity. The routing policy and the eval gate are the product.

This runs in your browser. Nothing you type leaves the page.

Confidence is a margin

The top intent’s keyword score divided by the top two scores combined. The invoice dispute scores 5 for shipment status and 1 for the runner-up, so 5 / (5 + 1) = 0.83: one intent clearly won, and it is the wrong one. “Where is the quote?” ties shipment status and quote request at 3 / (3 + 3) = 0.50, below the floor, so a person reads it.

The failure the gate caught

The switch reproduces the failure my first eval run caught: an invoice dispute classified as a status check and auto-answered. The fix was a rule: billing language goes to a human.

Why the gate is recall

A needless escalation costs a person a minute. A missed one puts a machine’s answer in front of an angry customer or a payment dispute. So the gate counts the second kind, and intent accuracy is reported, not gated. Switch the guardrail off and accuracy stays at 24/26 while the product becomes unshippable.

“Overall accuracy is the number people quote. Escalation recall is the number that decides whether you ship.”

FinOps and payables

The money side of a freight marketplace, from invoice to payout.

  • Milestone-based wallet and bank payouts, eliminating payment leakages
  • Regulatory-compliant invoicing: ZATCA, Uyumsoft, 1C
The problem and what I did

The problem. In freight, carriers want to be paid on delivery and customers pay on their own terms. The platform in the middle carries that gap, and every manual step in billing widens it.

What I did. As Senior Product Manager I delivered the FinOps module, with real-time financial dashboards and workflows. Writing

Arguments I keep making, written out in full.

  • Why agents need evals

    Essay, 4 minute read

    Most agent demos show you the run that worked. This essay is about the runs they leave out, and why accuracy is the wrong number to gate a release on.

  • Price the relationship, not the third decimal

    Essay, 5 minute read

    A quote nobody can take apart is a quote nobody trusts. Why every price should be line items a human can argue with, and why expected profit peaks at neither the highest win rate nor the fattest margin.

  • Money never floats

    Essay, 5 minute read

    What a double-entry ledger for carrier payouts teaches: derive balances, never store them, and check the trial balance per currency, because converting first hides real imbalances behind FX rounding.

How I work

The rules I keep coming back to, each with its reason.

“Ship the boring version first. The elegant version teaches you nothing until real users have broken the simple one.”

  • Evals before scale.Nothing an LLM touches meets a customer without a measured baseline and an escalation path for when the model is wrong.
  • Policy beats classification.A workflow should survive the model being wrong. The rules about who answers a customer live in plain code, where a wrong model cannot touch them.
  • Money logic must be explainable.If pricing or settlements cannot be walked through with a CFO on one screen, they are not done.
  • A theme without a quote is an opinion.If research cannot point to a customer’s own words, it is the researcher talking.
  • Process is a product too.If it takes forty slides to explain, the process is the problem, not the audience.
